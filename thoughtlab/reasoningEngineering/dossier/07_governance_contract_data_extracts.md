# Governance, data-stewardship, and contract extracts

**Compiled by:** Joint Recovery Council general counsel  
**Status:** authenticated excerpts and issue notes; not an ultimate legal opinion  
**Date:** 2 June 2026

## Joint Recovery Charter

The Council may establish binding exchange-wide standards for service continuity, security, interoperability, and financial control by supermajority vote. A supermajority requires the Benefits Authority, Treasury, the Cooperative, and at least two participating counties. The Council controls the shared modernization appropriation and may condition new program funding on compliance with approved standards.

Each member retains authority over its internal workflow, member-owned systems, personnel, and locally collected case information unless another law or signed agreement provides otherwise. Durable changes to a member’s workflow or data model require that member’s approval. The Council may designate an Incident Coordination Lead to direct temporary interface controls for no more than forty-five days when continued operation presents a material risk to households, providers, or public funds. The charter does not define whether migration sequencing is an interface control.

The Benefits Authority has statutory responsibility for statewide eligibility policy and for the validity of benefit determinations. Participating counties are statutory stewards of applicant-submitted facts and local case records. Treasury is responsible for lawful settlement and the final public accounts. Counsel notes that these duties overlap without creating one obvious owner for every intermediate event.

## Data-stewardship issue note

The Benefits Authority argues that its responsibility for valid determinations permits it to require one authoritative eligibility record and to direct the technical sequence needed to create it. Three counties agree that determinations must be authoritative but dispute that this permits the Authority to control their casework data model or operating platform. Treasury states that an eligibility determination cannot by itself redefine a settled payment.

Privacy agreements permit necessary operational exchange among members. They restrict creation of additional full-data copies unless a purpose, retention period, access model, and disposal process are approved. A time-limited shadow service may qualify if it does not initiate benefits and if reconciliation access is logged. Counsel has not determined whether a prolonged dual-production arrangement would remain within that approval.

## Tern Systems agreement

Acceptance of the New Case Platform is conditional for functions dependent on the unfinished replacement service. Contracted scenario passage does not waive defects hidden by unrepresentative test data. Tern has thirty days to cure material connector defects within its supported configuration. Custom extensions are excluded unless disclosed and accepted into the baseline. The parties dispute whether Eastbank’s timeout-reopen extension was merely disclosed or contractually accepted.

The current price includes transition operation through 31 March 2027. Continued mixed operation after that date permits a negotiated adjustment. Production-cohort milestones generate vendor fees, but the Council may withhold the portion attributable to an uncured material defect. Termination assistance includes structured data export, interface documentation, and up to twelve staff-weeks. It does not require Tern to recreate local workflow state in a competing product.

## Funding correspondence

The modernization appropriation assumes a major production milestone before the 2027 seasonal freeze. A grant officer wrote that the schedule may be re-baselined if the Council submits an integrated recovery plan, independent assurance, and a funded lifecycle path by 15 October 2026. The correspondence does not promise approval or additional money. Some program presentations call the production milestone mandatory; the underlying grant language allows amendment at the funder’s discretion.
