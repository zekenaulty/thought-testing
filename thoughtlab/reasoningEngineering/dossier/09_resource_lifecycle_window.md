# Resource, lifecycle, security, and delivery-window brief

**Joint submission by:** Council finance office, security office, workforce lead, and procurement lead  
**Status:** planning ranges, not vendor bids or approved option budgets  
**Date:** 1 July 2026

The Council’s currently approved modernization envelope can fund the committed baseline plus recovery work equal to approximately 18 percentage points of the remaining program envelope. Contingency funding may become available after the October recovery submission, but it is not committed.

The Legacy Benefits Core’s standard support ends 30 June 2027. A twelve-month limited extension is commercially available and would cover severe defects and infrastructure compatibility, not undocumented business behavior. Procurement estimates the extension and required hosting renewal at 7–11 percentage points of the remaining program envelope. The security office can accept the current privileged-access exception only through 31 March 2027 without new compensating controls, estimated at another 2–4 points including independent validation.

Seven people understand at least one of the three legacy transaction paths implicated in the incident. Two are eligible to retire in December, one contractor leaves in February, and three are already committed to annual rule changes. A retention package is possible but not approved. The replacement program needs the same specialists for rule mapping and verification, so legacy stabilization and replacement acceleration compete for scarce knowledge even when their direct budgets differ.

County Services Cooperative has sufficient infrastructure headroom for additional correlation and a time-limited shadow feed. Instrumenting all three legacy transaction paths and the bypass feeds so that commit state can be queried is estimated at 3–5 points and would require three legacy specialists plus four integration engineers for twelve to sixteen weeks. A durable conformance and reconciliation capability is estimated at 5–8 points beyond basic instrumentation and would require six additional engineers and a twenty-four-hour support rotation. The Council has funding for three positions this year. A narrower conformance service could be operated by the existing team if other planned gateway work is deferred.

Treasury can assign four analysts to transition and reconciliation for six months, but two are presently occupied by incident cleanup. Eastbank has twelve staff performing manual cross-system comparison and can sustain that level through October. Other counties have not budgeted comparable staff. Extending the current manual process network-wide is not feasible.

The annual policy-change release occurs in January. A seasonal service freeze prohibits mass workflow or payment cutover from 15 November 2026 through 15 February 2027, though monitored interface controls and shadow processing are permitted. The grant plan assumes a material production cohort by 31 March 2027. Missing that date does not end the program, but it makes amendment necessary and increases legacy-extension pressure.

Security regards long-lived duplicate stores as a material privacy and access risk. A bounded six-month shadow operation is estimated at 3–5 points if deletion and access controls remain within the present approval. A dual-production design would need a new privacy assessment and a clearly authoritative reconciliation process. Central consolidation could reduce the number of data copies but would increase the impact of one privileged boundary and may require new data-sharing approvals.

Incremental replacement acceleration is estimated at 8–12 points, mainly for rule completion, production-like testing, and specialist backfill. Preparing Eastbank for operational rollback is estimated at 2–4 points plus twenty-four temporary caseworkers for eight to ten weeks. These capability ranges are not automatically additive: scope and specialist use overlap, and vendor cure credits or contingency funding could change the Council-funded share.
