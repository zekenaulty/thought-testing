# Stakeholder hearing excerpts

**Source:** verified Joint Recovery Council transcript  
**Session:** 21 July 2026  
**Editing:** repetitive procedural remarks removed; speakers confirmed the retained excerpts

**Benefits Authority program director:** “We cannot delegate the validity of an eligibility determination to an integration queue. The Authority must be able to say which determination controls. That does not require counties to use one casework product: a replacement determination service could accept a governed case-fact contract from more than one workflow. To make that real before legacy support ends, I would have to commit four of our senior policy specialists at least half time and defer some annual-rule work.”

**Benefits Authority legacy operations manager:** “We tested what it would take to receive Eastbank cases through the former intake path. With the structured export and manual reconstruction, we could reopen intake over six weeks using twenty-four temporary caseworkers for eight to ten weeks. Queue order, pending document judgment, and worker intent would not all survive. That is operationally possible, but it is not pressing an undo button.”

**Non-pilot county director:** “Our county never used the new platform, yet provider corrections have produced mismatches for years. Do not make Eastbank the whole diagnosis. We will adopt a shared transaction contract. We will not replace our intake workflow on an emergency vote when the replacement exceptions are still unknown.”

**County Services Cooperative operations manager:** “The gateway had headroom during the incident. What it lacked was knowledge. We knew a request timed out; we did not know whether the business action committed. Instrumenting every legacy and bypass path before we can query that state is twelve to sixteen weeks with three legacy specialists and four integration engineers. Give us stable identity and queryable outcome state and we can stop unsafe retries. If you ask us to become the permanent financial truth layer, fund and govern that as a new service.”

**Treasury deputy controller:** “Exactly-once delivery is not the same as exactly-once obligation. We received distinct-looking authorizations that people later judged to be the same obligation. Treasury needs explicit reversal lineage and an authority for that judgment before settlement, not a nightly apology file.”

**Eastbank caseworker representative:** “The old process hid corrections in notes and made staff re-enter evidence. The new one is better for families when it works. After the incident, the screen said pending but nobody could tell us whether save meant received, decided, or paid. Some repeated submissions were rational responses to a meaningless status.”

**Provider association representative:** “Providers can tolerate a day’s delay if they know what is happening. Duplicate money is not a gift; it creates recovery notices, accounting work, and fear that later payments will be withheld. We care more about stable references and predictable correction than which product name is on the screen.”

**Privacy officer:** “Shadow processing can be justified to reduce uncertainty if it is bounded, logged, and deleted. Two live authoritative copies with indefinite reconciliation are a different proposition. Centralization also is not automatically private; it concentrates access and may cross purposes for which counties collected information.”

**Tern Systems delivery executive:** “The mixed design translates intent after semantics are lost. We can finish the replacement path, but not if every specialist is assigned to preserving undocumented legacy behavior. We accept responsibility for the connector default. We do not accept that a local extension outside the supported baseline proves the platform architecture failed.”

**Legacy technical lead:** “There is no single legacy behavior to preserve. One correction path commits before acknowledgement, another after, and the batch path is its own world. We can document and instrument them, but the people who know where to look are the same people the replacement team needs for rule validation.”

**Regulator observer:** “The Council must show control of public funds and continuity of lawful determinations. We do not require one vendor or one database. We do require identifiable responsibility while systems change, and documented action when a milestone, safeguard, or operating limit is missed.”

**Council finance chair:** “The recovery allocation is real, but it is not enough to purchase every form of safety simultaneously. Bring us a sequence with choices, not a list in which legacy extension, accelerated replacement, permanent reconciliation, full rollback, and dual operation all happen together.”

**Eastbank operations director:** “We can hold the current cohort and help produce evidence through October. We cannot keep thirty-eight people reconciling indefinitely, and we cannot promise that a technical data export restores the work state we left behind.”

The chair closed the hearing by requesting a recovery course that protects current service, assigns decision rights during transition, and gives the Council concrete actions when agreed milestones or safeguards are missed.
